To begin with, the financial effect that the CS:GO 'arms deal' update has had is that it is encouraging poor financial decisions for the players across the age range. This is something that is heavily regulated within the UK as they do not allow for underage gambling, however this is hard to enforce in an online situation as it is difficult to ensure those of age are the only ones accessing the sites. These sites themselves consist of betting skins that the player owns on their steam account against online tournaments or other minigames. The winning factor on these bets can include cash or higher value skins depending on the websites, however the mass majority are equipped with a 'cash-out' method, allowing players to sell of the earned skins for real money. As well as the reverse factor, using money to bet on winning different skins for their account. This has been an ongoing issue within this industry for years as it allows individual people to open their own gambling websites no matter who they are. the largest known controversy surrounding this point arose back in 2016, with three YouTubers (Trevor "TmarTn" Martin, Thomas "ProSyndicate" and Josh "JoshOG" Beaver) who would stream and post videos of them gambling on a site known as 'CS:GO Lotto' without exposing to the public that they are affiliated with the site, it was later discovered that Thomas and Trevor were listed as owner and co-owner of the company. While this isn't inherently an issue, the biggest problem with this was they were rigging their own games to make the gambling seem to cash out more than it actually would. As a result of this, a large quantity of viewers then visited this website expecting to also win big, but were met with a disappointment as they discovered that the game was rigged and they were unable to receive the same results as the Youtubers. As this is seen as a crime, they were on trial to face charges for this, although in the end it was settled out of court. However they were told to fully disclose all the details of their site and stream winning probability and valve clamped down on a variety of other sites doing the same. (https://www.espn.com/esports/story/_/id/16790802/gaming-personalities-embroiled-counter-strike-gambling-scandal). 
In direct correlation to the individual benefits from the gambling sites, valve also profits off of this, which is why they are refusing to clamp down more on the arms deal update and the gambling sites that have followed from this as a result even though there is a large amount of backlash from multiple governments all over the world. Valve takes a small amount of profit from all assets sold on their steam market, this is dependant on how much the item itself made (5% steam free and an additional 10% game fee for items from certain games such as CS:GO). (https://help.steampowered.com/en/faqs/view/61F0-72B7-9A18-C70B#steamfee). This means that the more that a skin sells for, the more profit Valve makes off of this item. Since the steam market is based off of drop rates and rarity of an item, Valve can gain a large amount of profit from little to no work off of the corporation itself. Even before the gambling sites became common, a large amount of people on CS:GO would choose to gamble in the game itself, buying keys for the crates which give them the opportunity to win these skins through a luck based system. Although many people still do, most choose to either gamble on external sites or buy from the steam market directly in order to attempt to save some money that otherwise would have been used to gamble for the skin in the game, meaning that they may have saved money (if the skin harder to get than its worth) or lost (if the skin is easier to gamble for rather than pay). 
Due to the nature of online games becoming more popular over lockdown, underage gambling has become more rampant than when CS:GO first released their "arms-deal update". While most people think of it as a harmless game of chance it is so much more than that to younger people who need new or 'the best' skins in order to show of to friends and people in the game. a large quantity of children and young adults are aware of underage gambling with the statistic rising in recent years. As of 2017, 45% of 11-16 year olds were aware of skin betting with 11% of them having placed bets using in game items. (https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/technology-42311533). To specify more about the effects that gambling has on young adults, a student in Bangor University began skin gambling at age 15, which led to him loosing over £2,000 four years later, he states that "I'd get my student loan, some people spend it on expensive clothes, I spend it on gambling virtual items". He kept this habit up throughout university, which inevitably led to him developing a gambling addiction. Studies suggest that gambling at a young age, pre frontal lobe development, can lead to sever and long lasting hasher gambling addictions due to the nature of addictive personality within adolescents. In an article talking about the lasting effects of gambling on younger generations, ESPN discussed the wider effect that a single person in the household gambling can have on family and friends directly linked to that person. Although gambling sites are aware of this issue, they continue to allow it to be advertised to young children by making brand deals with different YouTubers and live-streamers, such as Jeff (a YouTuber), who was offered $280,000 over two months for a small amount of 30 second advertising spots within his videos. Once gambling becomes a habit, many struggle to break it like any other addiction. Since both video games and gambling are within themselves addictions, many struggle with breaking just one and they tend to blend together as a large amount of people use gaming as an escape from work or school and fall back into gambling habits since they are already playing the game. If those with the online addiction find it doesn't have the same dopamine release after a while, they may turn to other forms of online gambling or in person gambling. 
Addiction isn't the only negative that comes out of video game gambling, there is also a rise of corruption within the gambling sites themselves. Some gambling sites allow players to bet on esports games and the players within them (such as how many kills a player will get) and are able to gain real money off of these bets. The issues began to arise when players began to rig the games by asking friends to bet on their kill count and purposely loosing the games after hitting a certain amount of kills or whatever else the bet requires of the player.
